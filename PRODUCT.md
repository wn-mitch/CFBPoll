# Product

## Register

product

## Users

College football fans who want a straight answer to "who is actually
best right now", checking in on a phone during a Saturday slate or on a
laptop early in the week. Many arrive skeptical of the human polls and want
evidence, not a hot take. A smaller group (stats-minded fans, people
settling an argument) digs into the method and the bias audit.

## Product Purpose

A predictive college football ranking: each team's expected margin against
an average FBS team, with honest uncertainty, built without preseason
reputation or conference bonuses. It also forecasts games and playoff odds,
and measures how the AP, Coaches and committee rankings differ from the
evidence. Success is a reader who leaves knowing where a team stands, how
sure the model is, and why it moved.

## Brand Personality

Plainspoken, evidence-first, quietly confident. Reads like a good
statistician explaining a result to a friend: no hype, no hedging, numbers
up front, uncertainty shown rather than hidden.

## Anti-references

- Sports-network pages: autoplay video, ad rails, hot-take headlines.
- Sportsbook UIs: neon on black, odds styled as calls to action.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: hero metrics, gradient accents, card grids.
- Pages that bury the ranking under explanation.

## Design Principles

1. The ranking is the product. The table loads first and reads at a glance;
   everything else is a click away.
2. Show uncertainty as a first-class number. Every rank and rating carries
   its range, in the same visual language across pages.
3. Explain movement. When a team moves, the reader can see which game moved
   it without leaving the page.
4. Earned familiarity. Standard tables, links and controls; no invented
   affordances.
5. Fair by construction. No conference or program gets visual emphasis the
   model does not give it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Win/loss and up/down are always paired with a letter or arrow,
never color alone. Charts carry text labels and a table or list
alternative. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Light and dark themes
follow the system setting.
